State of the Year Calendar

Engineering Model of Reality

Time is a continuous process. A process can be described by states. There are twenty-four states.

What is this

What state is the year in right now? This calendar answers one question.

A regular calendar tells you today's date. The state calendar tells you the state of the year. The year is a process like a project, a book, or a relationship: it has a beginning, a build-up, routine, need, pause, insight, and completion. The Engineering Model of Reality describes any process with twenty-four states — the year is simply the most accessible shared process there is.

This is a second dial. Next to "November 3" there is always a second answer: "Laguz, day thirteen."

The states are named after runes and carry their formulas from the series' rune map: Fehu — beginning, Hagalaz — living with the result, Dagaz — completion. In other words, the year speaks the same language the model uses to describe any of your processes. The calendar is not a separate thing — it is the system applied to the planet.

How it works

The zero is physical. The winter solstice: the moment when the light stops decreasing and turns around. This is measured, not chosen. In the system this is the node Blank Rune — the bifurcation point from Book 0, which now has a date and time. A node is an instant, not a day: on the circle it is a point, not a sector.

The grid is conventional — and we say so out loud. From the node the year is laid out into 24 equal-in-time states. The step ≈ 15.22 days. The grid divides the year by time, not by degrees, and could have been different — but the model's language is 24 states, and the system must speak one language at every scale.

The project's formula: the step is conventional, the zero is physical.

How to use it

Floor 1. Ten seconds. Look at the line above: the year is currently in such-and-such state, day so-and-so out of fifteen. That is already enough to know what time you are living in.

Floor 2. Two minutes at a node. Twenty-four times a year, every two weeks and a bit, the year switches. On that day ask yourself the window question from the table. It is like a sync with your team — except the team here is the planet.

Floor 3. Ten minutes, optional. Take the diagnostics and look at the "World × You" card: what state the year is in — and what stage you are at. These are different clocks, and being out of sync is normal (see the next block).

The calendar demands no belief and no discipline. It is like a milestone by the road: you can check it — or keep walking. The model is useful as long as it is useful.

The year and you

The world has a state. You have a stage. Different clocks.

If the year is in Fehu and you are in Hagalaz, that is not a "mismatch of energies" and not a breakdown. The world has started something new; you are living with the result. The scales are different, there is no friction. The "World × You" card is simply a map: it shows both positions and leaves the decision to you.

Example:

World: Laguz (21/24) — flow, trust: the world is already carrying.

You: Nauthiz — need, a signal: your resource is squeezed tight, and that is a message, not a breakdown.

Cross-check: the world's flow does not cancel your need; the need does not cancel the flow. The question of the window days: what can already be given to the flow — and what are you still holding for no reason?

Honest answers

Is this a horoscope?
No. The calendar predicts nothing and grades no days ("good/bad"). It shows the state of the process called "the year". The decisions are yours.
What do runes have to do with it?
In this series runes are not fate signs but names of process stages. Fehu is not "money" — it is a beginning; Hagalaz is not "destruction" — it is living with the result. The calendar simply applies this language to the year.
Why from the solstice and not from January 1?
January 1 is an accounting convenience. The solstice is a measurement: the moment when the light physically turns around. The zero point of the calendar must be measurable, not appointed.
Why 24 states and not 12 or 365?
Because the language of the model is 24 states, and the system speaks one language at every scale: day, year, project, life. The step is a convention; only the zero is physical.
Are there similar calendars?
Yes: humanity has divided the circle of 24 more than once — the Chinese 24 solar terms (on the UNESCO list), the rune "half-months" of the 20th century. Pleasant company, but not kinship: those systems answer their own questions ("what to do for the household", "what is my birth rune"). Our question is the state of the year, our zero is the measured moment of the solstice, our step is computable.
My state doesn't match the state of the year — am I doing something wrong?
No. These are different clocks. Knowing both readings is useful; forcing them into sync is not.
